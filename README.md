# Issues and Resolution - Pressure in Liquids
This document is a collection of issues that were found as a result of the study of pressure, pressure gauges, laws of pressure, accuracy and precision of pressure and performance metrics of pressure. 

Medium: Liquid. All of these issues are studied and explained specifically with respect to liquids. The example of a liquid used to help understand these issues is water.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Issue 1: Pressure Gauges measure Static Pressure and Gauge Pressure
A description of why pressure gauges are not a measure of anything and they are not measuring “Static or Gauge Pressure”.

### Issue 2: Pressure in fluids (liquids) = (Static Pressure, Dynamic Pressure)
A description of why pressure in liquids can never be “Static & Dynamic Pressure” and there can never be a “total pressure component” that equates to “Static + Dynamic Pressure”.

### Issue 3: Hydrostatic Pressure = ρgh
A description of why hydrostatic pressure is not a function of height and what does it actually depend on by explaining flow dynamics.

### Issue 4: Absolute Pressure = Gauge Pressure + Atmospheric Pressure
A description of how “Gauge Pressure” is not relative to “Atmospheric Pressure” and why the relation of “Gauge, Atmospheric, & Absolute Pressure” does not hold true. 

### Issue 5: Pressure is transmittable, Pascal’s Law
A description of why “Pressure” is not a transmittable quantity which forms the basis of Pascal’s Law and how “P” is unnecessary in “P = F/A”.

### Issue 6: Darcy Weisbach’s Equation of Pressure Loss in Pipes
A description of why Darcy’s equation of “Pressure losses” due to friction is first based on the wrong understanding of “Friction” and second not calculating any actual water loss in/ from pipes.

### Issue 7: Accuracy and Precision of Pressure Gauges
A description of why ASME and EN standards of accuracy and precision for “Pressure Gauges” are unnecessary based on what “Gauges” actually measure and why certain design standards for the accuracy of the “Gauges” actually are not driving towards a precise reading of “Static Pressure”.
